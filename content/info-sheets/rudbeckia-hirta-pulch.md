## _Rudbeckia hirta var. pulcherrima_

**Native to:** Species native across New England; this variety only in VT, MA

**Life cycle:** Biennial or Short-Lived Perennial

**Light**: Full/Partial Sun

**Soil Moisture:** Medium-Wet to Medium-Dry

**Height**: 1-2’<br />
**Spread**: 12-18”<br />
**Spacing**: 10-12”

**Blooms**: Jun-Oct<br />
**Bloom Color:** Yellow		

**Natural Habitat:** Dry meadows, roadsides, and disturbed areas

**Pairs Well With:** Butterfly milkweed, smooth blue aster, foxglove beardtongue, and sundial lupine

**Benefits**: Pollinator Favorite, Deer/Rabbit Resistant, Bird Favorite, Urban Environment, Drought Tolerant, Salt Tolerant, Host Plant

> “A bright golden-yellow meadow coneflower with a black center that blooms from mid-summer to fall. It attracts numerous butterflies when in bloom, and birds to its seed heads. Thrives in poor, dry soils. Will bloom the first year from seed; easy to establish in waste areas. A good urban plant for sunny dry locations.”
> 
> --Wild Seed Project

> “Black-eyed Susan is one of the larval host plants of the Silvery Checkerspot. Its bright yellow flowers attract many different butterflies, moths, and bees. This plant is of high value to pollinators.”
> 
> --Prairie Moon Nursery
