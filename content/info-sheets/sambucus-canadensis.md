### aka American black elderberry

## _Sambucus canadensis_

**Native to:** All of New England

**Life cycle:** Perennial Shrub

**Light**: Full/Partial Sun

**Soil Moisture:** Medium-Wet to Medium-Dry

**Height**: 5-10’<br />**Spacing**: 3-5’

**Blooms**: Spring<br />
**Bloom Color:** White

**Fruits**: Summer<br />
**Fruit Color:** Black, Purple

**Natural Habitat:** Wet meadows, hedgerows, and roadsides

**Benefits**: Pollinator Favorite, Bird Favorite, Deer Resistant, Host Plant (eg. polyphemus and cecropia moths), Compaction Tolerant, Urban Environment, Salt Tolerant, Edible, Low Maintenance

**Pairs Well With:** Winterberry holly (in mixed hedge), New England aster, New York ironweed, blue iris, and boneset

> "With wandering branches, broad corymbs of fluffy white flowers, and a beautiful late-season crop of dark berries, this shrub is as pleasing to wildlife as it is to humans. The fruit is edible, but should be cooked, dried, or fermented prior to consumption."
> 
> -- Native Plant Trust

> "A dramatic native shrub with arching branches covered with large flat-topped clusters of white flowers in summer followed by deep purple fruit. Bees are attracted to the flowers and stems provide overwintering habitat for pollinators' larvae. Birds and humans relish the fruits."
> 
> -- Wild Seed Project
