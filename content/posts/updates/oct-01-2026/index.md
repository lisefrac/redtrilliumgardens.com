## Milkweed Madness

Are your milkweeds looking, well... sad? 

Don't worry; ours are, too! Milkweeds are some of the earliest plants to go dormant in the fall, with the greens turning yellow, then brown, and then dying back. While all herbaceous perennials do this over the winter, none are quite so dramatic about it as milkweeds! One cold snap and they are like "guess I'll die" 🤷‍♀️

But they aren't dead -- only dormant! Beneath the surface are healthy roots, waiting for the days to lengthen and the soil to warm again to send up new shoots. 

I recognize, however, that they look kind of... dire. "If I put them in the ground now, will they really survive and return next year?" I hear you saying. 

I can understand the hesitation, and in response, *I'm marking down all milkweeds* from now until the end of the season. That includes poke milkweed (Asclepias exaltata), rose/swamp milkweed (A. incarnata), and whorled milkweed (A. verticillata). Pints are $6 and quarts are $10 -- basically, last year's prices. 

(Common milkweed -- A. syriaca -- is still available free with a purchase of $10 or more).

I'll be applying this to other species as they go dormant, too.
